CLASS 10 • CHAPTER 01

Life Processes

Nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion — concepts, processes and exam questions.

1. Life Processes — Overview

Life processes are the basic activities required to maintain an organism. In Class 10, the major life processes are nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion.

2. Nutrition

Nutrition provides materials and energy needed for growth, repair and maintenance. In autotrophic nutrition, organisms such as green plants prepare food using carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll. In heterotrophic nutrition, organisms obtain food from other organisms.

Human digestion involves ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion. The mouth begins digestion; the stomach provides an acidic medium and enzymes; the small intestine completes digestion and absorbs nutrients through villi; the large intestine mainly absorbs water.

3. Respiration

Respiration releases energy from food. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and generally releases more energy, while anaerobic pathways occur without oxygen. In humans, gas exchange occurs in the alveoli of the lungs. The respiratory system includes nasal passages, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles and lungs.

4. Transportation

In humans, blood transports oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, hormones and wastes. The heart pumps blood through pulmonary and systemic circulation. Red blood cells contain haemoglobin. Plants transport water and minerals through xylem and food through phloem.

5. Excretion

Excretion removes metabolic wastes. In humans, kidneys filter blood and form urine through processes including filtration, reabsorption and secretion. Plants remove or store wastes in several ways, including diffusion and storage in leaves or other parts.

Easy Questions

Q. What is nutrition?Ans. It is the process by which an organism obtains and uses food.
Q. Where does gas exchange occur in human lungs?Ans. Mainly across the thin walls of the alveoli.
Q. What is the function of xylem?Ans. It transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of a plant.

Exam-Level Questions

Q. Why is separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood important in humans?Ans. It helps maintain efficient oxygen supply by preventing significant mixing of oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood and supports the high energy requirements of the body.
Q. Explain the role of villi in the small intestine.Ans. Villi greatly increase the absorptive surface area and contain a rich network of blood vessels, helping digested nutrients enter the blood efficiently.
CLASS 10 • CHAPTER 02

Control and Coordination

Nervous system, reflex action, brain, plant hormones and animal hormones.

1. Coordination

Coordination enables an organism to detect changes and produce suitable responses. In animals it mainly involves the nervous system and endocrine system. Plants coordinate responses using chemical substances and growth movements.

2. Nervous System

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system. It receives and transmits information. A typical neuron has dendrites, a cell body and an axon. Information passes between neurons across synapses using chemical messengers.

3. Reflex Action

A reflex action is a quick, automatic response to a stimulus. Many reflexes are coordinated through the spinal cord, forming a reflex arc that allows a rapid response before conscious processing is completed.

4. Human Brain

The brain is protected by the skull and associated protective structures. Major regions include the forebrain, midbrain and hindbrain. Different areas help with thinking, sensory processing, movement, balance and involuntary functions.

5. Plant Coordination

Plants respond to stimuli through movements and chemical signals. Auxin is involved in growth responses such as phototropism. Other plant hormones include gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid and ethylene, each with characteristic roles.

6. Hormonal Coordination

Endocrine glands release hormones into the bloodstream. Hormones regulate processes such as growth, metabolism, reproduction and responses to stress. Examples include insulin, thyroxine, adrenaline and growth hormone.

Easy Questions

Q. What is a reflex action?Ans. A rapid and automatic response to a stimulus.
Q. What is the basic unit of the nervous system?Ans. Neuron.

Exam-Level Questions

Q. How does a reflex arc help an organism?Ans. It provides a rapid pathway for responding to a stimulus, helping reduce response time.
Q. How do hormones differ from nervous impulses?Ans. Nervous signals are generally rapid and travel through neurons, whereas hormones are chemical messengers transported through body fluids and often produce slower, longer-lasting effects.
CLASS 9 • CHAPTER 01

The Fundamental Unit of Life

Cell structure, organelles, transport and cell division — detailed foundation for Biology.

1. Cell — Basic Unit of Life

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms. Organisms may be unicellular or multicellular. Cells vary in shape and size according to their functions.

2. Plasma Membrane

The plasma membrane forms the boundary of the cell and is selectively permeable. It regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell. Diffusion is movement from higher concentration to lower concentration. Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.

3. Cell Wall

Plant cells have a rigid cell wall outside the plasma membrane. It provides support and protection and helps prevent the cell from bursting when water enters.

4. Nucleus

The nucleus contains genetic material and helps control cellular activities. Chromosomes carry genes, which contain hereditary information.

5. Cell Organelles

  • Mitochondria: sites of aerobic respiration and major energy release.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum: involved in synthesis and transport of materials.
  • Golgi apparatus: modifies, packages and helps transport substances.
  • Lysosomes: contain digestive enzymes and help break down materials.
  • Ribosomes: involved in protein synthesis.
  • Plastids: plant-cell organelles; chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis.
  • Vacuoles: storage structures; plant cells often have a large central vacuole.

6. Cell Division

Cell division is important for growth, repair and reproduction. Mitosis generally produces two genetically similar daughter cells, while meiosis is associated with formation of reproductive cells and reduces chromosome number.

Easy Questions

Q. Why is the plasma membrane selectively permeable?Ans. It permits some substances to cross while restricting others.
Q. What is the function of mitochondria?Ans. They are major sites of aerobic respiration and energy release.

Exam-Level Questions

Q. What happens to a plant cell in a hypotonic solution?Ans. Water enters the cell by osmosis. The cell becomes turgid because the cell wall resists excessive expansion.
Q. Why are lysosomes called digestive sacs?Ans. They contain enzymes that can digest worn-out cell components and certain foreign materials.
